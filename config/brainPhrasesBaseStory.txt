The city exhaled into the night, its lungs emptying as the streets fell still. Marta walked alone down the long avenue towards the park, hands buried deep in the pockets of her coat, her mobile silent for hours. Her latest affair had ended just like the rest: promises, followed by disappointment, then silence. She had sworn to herself she wouldn't fall for it again. Yet she had ventured out regardless, drawn by a text from a friend: *There’s a free party in the woods, come through.* She didn’t quite know why she was going. Perhaps merely to escape the suffocating quiet of her flat.
The air in the park was crisper, and beneath the sparse glow of the streetlamps, figures lingered on the benches—shadows laughing softly in the dark. Marta recognised Dario, an old friend, gay and effortlessly elegant even in the dead of night. She sat beside him without asking. "Fleeing the scene too?" he asked. "Something like that," she replied. They made small talk, but beneath the words lay something else: two distinct kinds of loneliness, recognising one another.
Dario guided her deeper into the woods, away from the lamplight. "You're looking for a spark, Marta," he murmured, his voice a low, velvet purr that brushed against her ear. "Something to make you feel alive. I know just the spot."
The canopy above blocked out the moon, leaving them in a heavy, velvet darkness. The air grew thick with the scent of damp earth, crushed pine, and an undeniable, electric tension. Dario stopped in a secluded clearing where a few tangled fairy lights cast a dim, golden glow. He gave her a knowing, wicked little smile, pressed a lingering kiss to her cheek, and melted back into the shadows.
Marta’s breath hitched. Leaning against the rough bark of an ancient oak was a man. Tall, broad-shouldered, with eyes that caught the faint light and held it. He didn't speak immediately. He just looked at her, his gaze tracing the curve of her neck, the rapid rise and fall of her chest beneath her coat. When he finally stepped closer, the sheer heat radiating from him made her knees weak.
"You look entirely too tense for a night like this," he murmured, his accent a rich, dark baritone that sent a shiver straight down her spine. He reached out, his knuckles lightly grazing her jawline. The touch was a sudden spark in the damp night air. Marta’s lips parted, a soft, involuntary sigh escaping her. The memory of her last dreary relationship evaporated, replaced by a raw, pulsing ache low in her belly.
His hand slid down to the nape of her neck, his fingers tangling in her hair, tilting her head back. "Tell me to stop," he whispered, his lips hovering mere millimetres from hers, his breath warm and tasting of gin and mint. "Tell me to walk away."
But Marta didn't want him to walk away. She wanted the noise in her head to stop. She wanted to feel. Closing the distance, she pressed her mouth to his. The kiss was instantaneous and consuming—hungry, desperate, and utterly devoid of polite hesitation. His arms wrapped around her waist, pulling her flush against his hard body, lifting her slightly so her toes barely touched the mossy ground.
Every nerve ending in her body caught fire. His hands explored the curve of her spine, pressing her closer, his mouth moving against hers with a slow, deliberate rhythm that made her gasp into his mouth. The cool night air contrasted sharply with the scorching heat of his skin. She tangled her fingers in his hair, pulling him deeper, chasing the friction, chasing the thrill of being entirely, wonderfully consumed by the dark.
